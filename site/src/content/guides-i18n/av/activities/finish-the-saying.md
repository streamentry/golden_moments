summary: >-
  Finish-the-saying games read a familiar phrase and pause before the last
  word, such as "Don't count your chickens before they..." The person fills in
  the ending, or chooses it from two or three options. Use sayings the person
  grew up with, offer choices, and treat any answer as a good start to a talk,
  never as right or wrong. Golden Moments has a free finish-the-saying game you
  can play on a tablet or computer.
keyPoints:
  - "Use sayings from the person's own life and culture, and offer a choice of endings when open answers are hard."
  - "Say the first part slowly, pause, and wait. Do not fill in the word quickly, and do not quiz."
  - "Treat any answer as a good start. Say 'That's a good one' and talk about it, rather than correcting."
  - "Proverbs can be abstract. Keep to plain, familiar sayings for people in the middle and later stages."
  - "Stop when interest fades. Five to ten minutes of sayings is a full, enjoyable session."
faq:
  - q: "What is a finish-the-saying game for dementia?"
    a: "It is a game that reads a familiar saying up to its last word, then invites the person to finish it. People can say the ending aloud, point to a choice, or simply enjoy hearing the saying. Golden Moments offers a free version that shows choices on screen and reveals the ending gently."
  - q: "Is finish-the-saying a memory test?"
    a: "It can feel like one if it is run like a quiz. Play it as a shared, relaxed moment, offer choices, skip any answer that causes distress, and never show a score. The aim is enjoyment and conversation."
  - q: "Which sayings work best for seniors with dementia?"
    a: "Short, familiar sayings about everyday life tend to work best: weather, kitchen and home sayings, and sayings about family or the seasons. Sayings from the person's youth are often easiest to recognize. Avoid obscure proverbs, which can be confusing."
  - q: "What if the person gives a different ending?"
    a: "Accept it warmly and ask what it means to them. Many people give a different but reasonable ending, or one from a different saying. Use it as a way to start talking about their life."
