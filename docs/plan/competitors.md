# Competitors and review gaps

Researched 2026-10-30 and 31. US App Store pages were read directly where
possible (ratings, languages and versions as shown on 2026-10-31). Google Play
pages could not be fetched, so Play figures come from aggregator snippets and
are marked. Several App Store "latest version" dates omit the year. This is a
gap log, not proof of a blue ocean; recheck a live listing before quoting it.

## 2. Review-gap log

1. **Ads and scam in-app purchases cause panic.** A caregiver asks for "100% ad-free apps that only do what they say they do"; relatives install scam cleaner apps after ads; "$50/week" subscriptions ([Tildes, 2024](https://tildes.net/~health.mental/1k69/i_am_looking_for_100_ad_free_apps_for_older_adults_with_dementia_things_like_jigsaw_puzzles)).
2. **Subscription cost is the top stated drawback** for Memory Lane Games and Constant Therapy; GrandPad's $40/month is "a real pain point" ([ElderLivingHub](https://elderlivinghub.com/reviews/grandpad-tablet-review/)).
3. **Setup and login too hard for the participant** (MindMate aggregator reviews).
4. **Online dependency** breaks modules on weak Wi-Fi (AlzBuddy; Memory Lane Play snippets).
5. **Text and contrast too small** (Japanese store; MindMate "thin fonts").
6. **Content pitched wrong: childish or too hard.** "Toddler-like, wanted a simpler, more adult-looking game" (Alz App); "too difficult for middle stage"; "not for later stages".
7. **Repetition and thin libraries** ("same workout for 3 weeks"; "questions may feel too basic").
8. **Abandonment.** "Why isn't the app removed from the App Store?" (German store); removals; stale apps.
9. **Consistency matters; moved icons confuse** ([Alzheimer's Society forum](https://forum.alzheimers.org.uk/threads/using-tablets-to-help-people-with-dementia.80545/)).
10. **Wants familiar content beyond games**: family photos, old films, music, video calls (same forum); Taiwan experts cite old songs, photos and stories ([udn](https://health.udn.com/health/story/10697/2646226)).
11. **Android requested** (Japanese review; SingFit is iOS-only).
12. **Crashes and "doesn't work"** one-star reviews.
13. **Category-level quality is low:** 17 dementia apps scored a uMARS mean of 3.35 of 5, with engagement and information lowest ([JMIR Formative 2024](https://formative.jmir.org/2024/1/e51076)).

What this means for us: ad-free, no subscription, no account, offline and adult-looking is exactly the gap people describe. It is also already true of the app and not yet the lead message anywhere. The gaps we do not fill are content scale and languages.

## 3. What the best-rated apps do that Golden Moments does not

- **Content scale**: Memory Lane Games' "thousands of games on hundreds of topics" against our eight packs; a care-home edition with ORCHA certification ([case study](https://healthinnovation-em.org.uk/images/EMDHA_case_study_-_MLG.pdf)).
- **Languages**: Memory Bond 11, Forget-Me-Not 21, Alzheimer & YOU 22. Tiny apps already cover FR, DE, ES, JA and KO, almost certainly by machine translation. Language count is table stakes; translation quality, local content and a localized listing are the difference.
- **Voice**: family voice recordings (Memory Bond and the Korean sticker app above). Deferred for us by decision.
- **Face cropping and "Who is this?" quizzes**: forced recall, which conflicts with our boundaries; not copied.
- **Helper mode behind a PIN, settings timeout**: keeps the participant out of settings and purchases (Forget-Me-Not, Recollect). Worth a light version that does not use a password, which the Dutch requirements study warns against.
- **Accessibility toggles** beyond large tiles: high contrast and color-vision modes (Forget-Me-Not).
- **Music and singing** (SingFit, Music & Memory); a **digital picture-frame mode** (Recollect).
- **An explicit "not a medical device" line in the listing** (Memory Bond, Constant Therapy); Google Play requires it for health apps.
- **Visible developer replies to reviews**: a cheap trust signal.

Not worth copying: score dashboards, adaptive difficulty, prevention or brain-training claims.

## 4. Medical-claim rules

- **Apple App Store Review Guidelines** ([source](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)): 1.4.1 medical apps that could be used for diagnosing or treating are reviewed with greater scrutiny and should remind users to check with a doctor; 5.1.1(ix) apps in highly regulated fields such as healthcare must come from a legal entity; 5.1.3 health-data rules. Choosing the *Medical* category invites 1.4.1 scrutiny; the game peers sit in Family, Health & Fitness or Education. We are in Games / Puzzle.
- **Google Play health apps policy** ([source](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12261419)): a health apps declaration; no false or misleading health claims; non-device health apps must say the app "is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition" and remind users to consult a professional.
- **FDA General Wellness policy, final, 2026-01-06** ([PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/90652/download)): software for a healthy lifestyle unrelated to diagnosis, cure, mitigation, prevention or treatment is not a device. Allowed wellness claims include relaxation and "mental acuity, concentration, problem-solving, pattern recognition" with no disease reference; a disease may be mentioned only as "as part of a healthy lifestyle, may help living well with [condition]". Trigger wording for a dementia game: "slows or prevents cognitive decline", "treats symptoms", "improves memory in people with Alzheimer's", "reduces agitation", "screens for dementia".
- **FTC**: Lumosity 2016, $2 million, for claims it would delay cognitive decline and protect against dementia ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges-its-brain-training-program)); Health Products Compliance Guidance 2022; penalty-offense notices to about 670 health marketers in 2023; the Prevagen memory-claim verdict 2024. Memory claims draw the same standard for apps as for supplements.
- **EU MDR**: MDCG 2019-11 revision 1 (June 2025) qualifies software by stated intended purpose; general wellness software is excluded (secondary sources; primary PDF not opened).
- **UK MHRA** stand-alone software guidance ([PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/1105233/Medical_device_stand-alone_software_including_apps.pdf)): the medical purpose is determined by the labeling and promotional material, explicitly including the app store description and category; general well-being is not usually a medical purpose; "alleviation" includes reducing symptoms.

Consequence for every locale: the translated store copy carries the "not a medical device" sentence, mentions dementia only to name the audience, and is checked against a per-language claims word-list before export.
